# Finance Business Partnering Battle Cards v2 — Decision Matrix

Date: 2026-08-30  
Scope: Commercial finance conversations with Marketing, Sales and Supply Chain.

All VietNova amounts and thresholds are synthetic or proxy-derived. The cards demonstrate the decision process; they do not claim realized savings or business impact.

## Finance response architecture

Use the same five-step structure in every meeting:

1. Clarify the commercial objective.
2. Rebuild the economic bridge from revenue to contribution profit and cash.
3. Quantify the minimum evidence required.
4. Offer a conditional path with a guardrail.
5. Escalate only when a threshold, policy or liquidity limit is breached.

## Decision matrix

| Request | Revenue upside is credible when | Finance value equation | Minimum approval evidence | Guardrail | Escalation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing +15% | Incremental, non-cannibalised orders are isolated | Incremental CM − CAC − trade/fulfilment spend | Matched baseline, channel economics, repeat-rate plan | LTV/CAC ≥ 1.0x; ROI ≥ 25% | Margin exception or risk-cap breach |
| Deeper customer discount | Volume and mix are contractually committed | Incremental CM after discount − cost-to-serve − cash cost | Customer P&L, volume floor, payment commitment | CM% ≥ 25%; DSO ≤ plan + 5 days | Negative incremental contribution |
| Higher inventory buffer | Service-level loss is costlier than carry cost | Avoided lost CM − carrying cost − markdown risk | SKU forecast, lead time, aging and release plan | Service level ≥ 95%; DIO within case | Downside DIO breach or revolver draw |

## Card A — Marketing asks for 15% more budget

### Finance position

Do not approve a blanket increase. Approve a capped test tranche only for channel-event combinations that clear the contribution hurdle after platform fees, discounts, returns and fulfilment.

### Counter-questions

- What is incremental revenue versus pull-forward or cannibalisation?
- What is contribution after channel fee and trade spend?
- What CAC and repeat-rate threshold must be reached?
- What is the stop-loss if payback exceeds the agreed window?

### Conditional offer

Start with a tranche capped at 20% of the current D2C budget. Release the next tranche only after a matched baseline/control-period readout. Track contribution, not clicks or gross sales.

### Stop / scale rule

- Scale when incremental CM is positive, ROI is at least 25% and LTV/CAC is at least 1.0x.
- Stop when CAC breaches payback hurdle, return rate exceeds limit or contribution becomes negative.
- Escalate if the transfer breaks the approved commercial risk cap.

## Card B — Sales requests a deeper strategic-customer discount

### Finance position

Evaluate the customer P&L after discount, rebates, returns, service cost, logistics and working-capital burden. A strategic logo does not exempt the account from an economic floor.

### Counter-questions

- Is the volume incremental or shifted from another channel?
- What payment terms and DSO impact accompany the offer?
- Can the customer commit to minimum volume, premium mix or payment date?
- Is the discount temporary, conditional and reversible?

### Conditional offer

Use a time-bound rebate linked to volume, mix and payment-date commitments. Show the pre/post customer contribution bridge separately; do not bury cost-to-serve in corporate overhead.

### Stop / scale rule

- Scale only if contribution margin remains at or above the 25% hurdle.
- Stop if incremental contribution turns negative or DSO exceeds plan by more than 5 days.
- Escalate any permanent list-price reset or strategic exception.

## Card C — Supply Chain requests a higher inventory buffer

### Finance position

Test service benefit against carrying cost, obsolescence, expiry and cash. Approve only for hero SKUs with a documented demand signal, not a blanket portfolio increase.

### Counter-questions

- Which SKU and service-level loss does the buffer solve?
- What incremental DIO and cash are tied up?
- What is the expiry, reserve or markdown exposure?
- Can cadence, allocation or supplier lead time solve the issue more cheaply?

### Conditional offer

Time-box the buffer to the peak window, fund it by reducing slow-moving SKUs, and set a release date with weekly review.

### Stop / scale rule

- Scale only when service level is at least 95% and DIO remains within the approved case.
- Stop replenishment for slow SKUs when aging or markdown risk breaches policy.
- Escalate if the request pushes DIO to the downside case or requires revolver funding.

## Meeting scorecard

| Question | Green | Amber | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contribution margin | ≥25% hurdle | 20–25% with documented strategic case | <20% or negative |
| Promotion ROI | ≥25% | 0–25% pending test | Negative |
| LTV/CAC | ≥1.0x | 0.8–1.0x with retention plan | <0.8x |
| DSO movement | Within plan | +0–5 days | >+5 days |
| DIO / service | Service ≥95% within case | Temporary exception | Downside breach / liquidity draw |
| Evidence | Reconciled and approved | One missing field with owner | Missing source, version or owner |

## Escalation path

- Green: owner can execute within approved budget and guardrails.
- Amber: Finance Business Partner documents exception, expected value and expiry date; CFO informed.
- Red: pause spend/discount/replenishment and escalate to CFO or investment committee.
- Every exception requires a review date and a documented reversal condition.

## Interview conversion

A strong answer is:

> I would not reject the commercial objective; I would change the approval rule. I would separate incremental revenue from contribution and cash, offer a capped test, define the threshold and owner, and stop or escalate when the guardrail fails. The figures are synthetic, so I would replace them with approved actuals, budget versions and a reconciled customer/channel P&L before calling the decision production-ready.
