# Finance Business Partner Battle Cards v1

These cards turn the model into a management conversation. All amounts are illustrative synthetic outputs from VietNova model v2, not company-reported facts.

## 1. Marketing asks for 15% more budget

### Request

Approve an additional 15% marketing budget for D2C and Marketplace to accelerate acquisition and reach.

### Finance response

Do not approve a blanket increase. Route incremental spend to the channel-event combinations that clear the contribution hurdle after platform fees, discount leakage, returns and variable fulfilment cost.

### Counter-questions

- What is incremental revenue versus pull-forward or cannibalised volume?
- What is the observed contribution after channel fee and trade spend?
- What CAC and repeat-rate threshold must be reached?
- What is the stop-loss trigger if contribution payback exceeds the agreed window?

### Acceptable range and guardrail

- Start with a controlled test tranche capped at 20% of current D2C budget.
- Require positive incremental contribution in the pre-evaluation simulator.
- Release the next tranche only after a matched baseline or control-period readout.
- Stop if CAC rises above the payback hurdle or if return rate exceeds the operating limit.

### Compromise

Fund the highest marginal-ROI channel first, keep a reserve for a second test, and report weekly on incremental contribution rather than clicks or gross sales.

### Escalation

Escalate to CFO if the request requires a margin exception, a new attribution method or a budget transfer that breaks the approved risk cap.

## 2. Sales asks for deeper discount to retain a strategic customer

### Request

Offer a deeper discount to protect volume and prevent a strategic customer from switching.

### Finance response

Evaluate customer profitability after discount, returns, service cost, logistics and working-capital burden. Revenue growth alone is not sufficient evidence of value creation.

### Counter-questions

- What is the customer contribution margin before and after the proposal?
- Is the volume incremental or simply shifted from another channel?
- What payment terms and DSO impact accompany the discount?
- Can the customer commit to a minimum volume, mix or payment behaviour?

### Acceptable range and guardrail

- Tie any discount to a volume floor, mix target and payment-date commitment.
- Keep contribution margin above the channel hurdle unless CFO approves an explicit strategic exception.
- Track returns and cost-to-serve separately; do not hide them in corporate overhead.

### Compromise

Use a temporary, conditional rebate with a quarterly review rather than a permanent list-price reset.

### Escalation

Escalate if the proposal creates a negative incremental contribution or extends DSO beyond the cash plan.

## 3. Supply Chain asks for a higher inventory buffer

### Request

Raise safety stock to avoid stock-outs during the seasonal peak.

### Finance response

Test the service benefit against carrying cost, obsolescence risk and cash release. Separate strategic hero SKUs from slow-moving or test SKUs.

### Counter-questions

- Which SKU and service-level problem does the buffer solve?
- What is the incremental DIO and cash tied up?
- What is the expiry, reserve or markdown risk?
- Can production cadence, allocation or supplier lead time solve the issue more cheaply?

### Acceptable range and guardrail

- Approve incremental buffer only for hero SKUs with a documented demand signal.
- Set an expiry/aging review and a release date after the peak.
- Keep the working-capital plan within the CCC guardrail.

### Compromise

Use a time-bound buffer for the peak window, fund it with a reduction in slow-moving SKUs, and review the release schedule weekly.

### Escalation

Escalate if the request pushes DIO above the downside case or requires a revolver draw.
