# MCH Financial Statement Analysis & Earnings Quality — FY2016–FY2025

**Review date:** 2026-08-30  
**Evidence class:** calculated from the approved MCH FY2016–FY2025 statement supplement; not an investment recommendation.

## Analyst question

Is reported profit growth translating into durable returns and cash generation, and which balance-sheet or cash-conversion signals deserve follow-up?

## Method

For each fiscal year:

- **Net margin** = PAT / net revenue.
- **Asset turnover** = net revenue / average total assets; FY2016 uses ending assets because no prior-year row is in this layer.
- **Equity multiplier** = average total assets / average owners' equity; FY2016 uses ending balances.
- **DuPont ROE** = net margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier.
- **ROA** = PAT / average total assets.
- **ROE** = PAT / average owners' equity.
- **CFO / revenue** = operating cash flow / net revenue.
- **Debt / equity proxy** = (total assets − owners' equity) / owners' equity.

The source facts are page-reviewed and the derived ratios are deterministic. FY2017 remains a comparative/corresponding-column year.

## Findings

1. **The quality of the earnings cycle changed by regime.** Net margin fell to 17.0% in FY2017, recovered to 22.0% by FY2019, reached 25.6% in FY2024 and then declined to 22.1% in FY2025. This supports a margin-regime discussion, not a straight-line growth claim.
2. **FY2025 ROE remains high but is leverage-sensitive.** Calculated ROE is 45.11% on average equity, with an equity multiplier of 2.06x and debt/equity proxy of 0.82x. The result should be read together with cash conversion and the capital base, not as a standalone quality score.
3. **Cash conversion is the primary FY2025 red flag.** CFO/revenue fell from 29.86% in FY2024 to 6.98% in FY2025; CFO/PAT in the companion trend layer fell to 31.5%. The correct management follow-up is an AR, inventory, payable and one-off cash-flow bridge.
4. **FY2024 ROE is an exception requiring note-level review.** ROE rises to 42.02% while owners' equity is materially lower than FY2023; the ratio is mathematically reproducible but economically sensitive to the equity base. No causal explanation is asserted without the relevant equity/restructuring notes.
5. **Efficiency recovered after the FY2022 slowdown.** Asset turnover moved from 0.85x in FY2022 to 0.99x in FY2025, while net margin remained above FY2022. This is a calculated signal of improving utilization, not proof of operational causality.

## Decision-oriented follow-up

| Signal | Management question | Evidence needed | Action if confirmed |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2025 CFO/revenue 6.98% | Which working-capital accounts absorbed cash? | CFO note, AR ageing, inventory and AP movements | owner-level cash release plan |
| FY2024 ROE 69.92% | Did equity change reflect dividend, restructuring or another event? | equity roll-forward and notes | restate/annotate ROE bridge |
| Debt/equity proxy 0.82x in FY2025 | Is debt service covered through the weak cash year? | debt maturity, interest and covenant schedule | liquidity stress and headroom test |
| FY2022 asset-turnover dip | Was the slowdown mix, capacity or demand driven? | volume/mix and asset utilization evidence | scenario input, not retrospective causality |

## Recruiter-safe takeaway

“I decomposed a ten-year audited-statement series into margin, efficiency and capital-structure drivers, then used CFO/revenue and equity-base diagnostics to identify the FY2025 cash-conversion follow-up instead of treating PAT growth as sufficient evidence.”

## Caveats

- FY2017 uses audited FY2018 comparative/corresponding columns.
- Ratios are calculated, not reported KPIs.
- Debt/equity is a balance-sheet proxy, not a substitute for the debt schedule.
- FY2024 ROE and FY2025 cash conversion require note-level follow-up before causal language or a valuation conclusion.
