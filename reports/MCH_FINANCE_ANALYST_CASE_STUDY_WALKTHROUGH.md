# MCH Finance Analyst Case Study Walkthrough (FY2016–FY2025)

## Executive answer

Masan Consumer Corporation (MCH) shows a decade of durable reported growth but a clear FY2025 quality-of-earnings warning:

- Net revenue increased from VND 13,789.8bn in FY2016 to VND 30,556.5bn in FY2025, a descriptive CAGR of 9.24%.
- Operating margin improved from 22.9% to 25.4% over the same period, with the series peak around 29% before FY2025.
- FY2025 revenue declined 1.1% year over year, while profit after tax declined 14.6%; PAT margin compressed by roughly 3.5 percentage points versus FY2024.
- Operating cash flow / PAT fell from approximately 116.5% in FY2024 to 31.5% in FY2025. This is the primary finance question: what changed in working capital, tax, supplier terms or other operating cash items?

The correct analyst conclusion is not “MCH is broken.” It is: **profit resilience is visible over the decade, but FY2025 requires a cash-conversion and margin-bridge review before approving an aggressive growth plan.**

## 1. Decision question

If I were supporting a CFO or business-finance lead, I would frame the review around three decisions:

1. Should FY2026 planning assume a normalised margin or reset to a more conservative base?
2. Is the FY2025 profit decline primarily volume/mix, gross-margin pressure, operating-cost deleverage, or a non-operating item?
3. Can management fund growth internally, or should cash-release actions be prioritised before incremental trade spend and capex?

## 2. Evidence and scope

The supplement uses official MCH audited consolidated financial statements and records:

- net revenue, gross profit, operating profit, profit before tax, profit after tax;
- total assets, owners' equity and operating cash flow;
- source document, printed page anchor, extraction status, confidence and comparability note.

All statement values are reported VND amounts. Margins, CAGR, YoY changes, CFO/PAT, equity ratio and ROA proxy are derived calculations.

FY2017 is explicitly tagged as an **audited comparative / corresponding-column extraction** from the official FY2018 filing. It is not presented as an independently located standalone FY2017 PDF. MCH is maintained as a separate supplement and is not silently merged into the 240-row VNM/QNS/KDC normalized peer core.

## 3. Analytical method

The review follows a finance-analyst workflow:

1. Tie each reported line to a filing page anchor.
2. Recalculate derived ratios from the reported numerators and denominators.
3. Compare the latest year with both the prior year and the decade base year.
4. Separate what the data proves from what requires a management follow-up.
5. Convert the result into a planning action, owner and next review.

Key definitions:

- Gross margin = gross profit / net revenue.
- Operating margin = operating profit / net revenue.
- PAT margin = profit after tax / net revenue.
- CFO/PAT = operating cash flow / profit after tax.
- Equity ratio = owners' equity / total assets.
- ROA proxy = PAT / total assets; this is not an average-balance ROA.

## 4. Trend findings

### Growth

Revenue grew from VND 13,789.8bn in FY2016 to VND 30,556.5bn in FY2025. The path is not linear: FY2020–FY2021 delivered strong expansion, FY2022 softened, and FY2024–FY2025 is a mature-scale period. A descriptive 9.24% CAGR is useful for context, but it should not be used as a mechanical FY2026 forecast.

### Margin

Gross margin stayed in a relatively tight mid-40% range, suggesting the long-run gross-profit engine is durable. Operating margin expanded from 22.9% in FY2016 to 25.4% in FY2025, but FY2025 fell from approximately 29.2% in FY2024. That decline implies the planning discussion should separate gross-margin pressure from opex deleverage rather than treating “margin” as one line.

### Profit quality

FY2025 PAT was VND 6,764.1bn, down 14.6% year over year, while revenue was down only 1.1%. The earnings decline therefore requires a bridge across:

- gross profit / price-volume-mix;
- selling, general and administrative cost;
- distribution and trade-spend intensity;
- other operating or non-operating items;
- tax-rate movement.

The current supplement deliberately does not invent that bridge when only statement-level data is available.

### Cash conversion

CFO/PAT was approximately 106.3% in FY2016, 101.8% in FY2020, 116.5% in FY2024 and 31.5% in FY2025. FY2025 operating cash flow of VND 2,132.3bn is therefore materially weaker than accounting profit of VND 6,764.1bn.

This is a diagnostic signal, not a causal conclusion. The next CFO pack should decompose the gap into receivables, inventories, payables, taxes, provisions and other operating assets/liabilities. Until that decomposition is available, the planning recommendation should include a cash guardrail and a working-capital action register.

## 5. Planning recommendation

### Base case

- Reset near-term operating margin toward a conservative level below FY2024's peak.
- Fund trade spend only where contribution margin and payback clear the hurdle.
- Use a weekly cash dashboard until CFO/PAT recovers toward a normalised range.
- Require a driver bridge for every material FY2025-to-FY2026 change.

### Upside case

Release upside only if three gates pass:

1. revenue growth is supported by sell-out or customer-level evidence;
2. incremental contribution margin remains above the hurdle after trade spend and channel cost-to-serve;
3. working-capital absorption is funded and collection/inventory actions have owners.

### Downside case

Stress-test a flat-to-down revenue environment, lower operating margin and slower cash conversion. The decision is not simply to cut all growth; it is to protect high-return channels and defer low-payback spend.

## 6. Interview-ready talk track

> “I built a ten-year MCH statement supplement from official filings. The headline is durable revenue growth and long-run margin expansion, but FY2025 is a quality-of-earnings checkpoint: revenue fell about 1%, PAT fell about 15%, and CFO/PAT dropped to roughly 32% from 117%. I would not claim the cause from statement-level data alone. I would take the bridge into receivables, inventory, payables, tax and trade spend, then set a cash guardrail for the next forecast cycle.”

## 7. What I would request next

To move from a high-quality public-finance review to a full operating diagnosis, request:

- monthly revenue and gross-profit bridge by category/channel;
- AR ageing, inventory days and AP ageing;
- trade-spend and promotion ROI by customer;
- tax and one-off reconciliation;
- capex and supplier-term changes;
- FY2026 budget assumptions with owner and source.

## 8. Limitations

This case study is intentionally transparent:

- FY2017 provenance is comparative/corresponding-column based.
- Ratios are derived, not management-reported KPIs.
- CFO/PAT is a screening ratio, not a substitute for a full cash-flow bridge.
- No causal attribution is made without granular operating data.
- The MCH supplement is separate from the normalized peer core for comparability control.
