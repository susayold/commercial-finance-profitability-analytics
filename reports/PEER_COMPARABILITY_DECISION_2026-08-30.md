This is a comparability decision, not a data fill-in. Blank or non-comparable metrics remain blank.

## What can be used in the portfolio

### VNM — structural benchmark

Use revenue CAGR, margin regime, cash conversion, capex intensity and asset efficiency. Keep the 2021–2025 summary layer visibly separate from the statement-verified 2016–2020 layer and retain the FY2006–FY2025 long-run panel's restatement/basis-break flags.

### QNS — input-cost and segment context

Use 2025 margins and the 2016–2020 statement layer for plant economics, sugar/soy input sensitivity and working-capital discussion. Label the 2021–2025 summary values as reported-summary evidence. Do not rank QNS on a full-period CAGR beside VNM.

### KDC — perimeter and one-off context

Use 2025 margins, asset efficiency and the 2016–2020 statement layer to discuss portfolio change, one-offs and perimeter risk. Present the revenue jump around the Vocarimex/TAC consolidation as reported growth, not organic growth.

### MCH — commercial anchor

Use official category/channel disclosures and the audited archive to calibrate the fictional VietNova commercial story. Keep OCR candidates in the human-review workbench; no candidate becomes verified solely because it is machine-selected.

## Required controls before expanding the comparable trend panel

1. Obtain statement-level FY2021–FY2025 QNS and KDC values for the same line-item basis.
2. Rebuild 2016–2025 revenue on one documented basis; retain original and normalized values side by side.
3. Document perimeter changes and calculate an adjusted bridge only where supportable.
4. Complete MCH page-level review for every metric used in a peer comparison.
5. Re-run the peer validator and update the evidence matrix before changing any CV claim or headline ranking.

## Reviewer conclusion

The current panel is strong enough for a finance-analyst portfolio because it demonstrates judgement about what **not** to compare. VNM supports long-run trend claims; QNS and KDC support bounded context and stress analysis. A single cross-company CAGR table that ignores revenue basis or perimeter changes would be less credible than the scoped decision above.
