# MCH Credit Memo — Finance Analyst Portfolio Case

**As-of:** 2026-08-30  
**Issuer:** Masan Consumer Corporation (MCH)  
**Purpose:** lender-style screening exercise using public audited statements; not a live credit rating or investment recommendation.

## Executive credit view

**Preliminary view: WATCH / CONDITIONAL SUPPORT.**

MCH has a strong reported capital base and a decade of profitable operations, but FY2025 cash conversion weakened sharply. Owners' equity was VND 18,662.1bn against total assets of VND 33,966.2bn, implying a 54.9% equity ratio. The main risk is not a proven solvency event; it is whether the FY2025 operating cash-flow shortfall reflects temporary working-capital absorption or a structural change in collections, inventory or supplier funding.

A lender should support ordinary-course funding only with enhanced information rights and a cash-conversion action plan. Do not approve an aggressive incremental facility from PAT alone.

## 1. Borrower and evidence boundary

MCH is the public-company anchor for the portfolio. The analysis uses the separate page-reviewed statement supplement:

- FY2016–FY2025, eight metrics per year;
- audited consolidated statements for FY2016 and FY2018–FY2025;
- FY2017 from the audited FY2018 comparative/corresponding columns;
- reported VND values with source page anchors and confidence/status fields.

The 240-row VNM/QNS/KDC normalized peer core is intentionally separate. The 59-row MCH OCR workbench remains intake-only and is not used as approved credit evidence.

## 3. Five-Cs assessment

### Capacity

Historical operating profit and PAT are substantial, and the decade shows positive profitability. However, FY2025 operating cash flow covered only 31.5% of PAT. Debt-service capacity cannot be concluded without the debt schedule, interest expense and maturity profile.

### Capital

The FY2025 equity ratio of 54.9% is a meaningful structural buffer. Total liabilities derived as assets less equity are approximately VND 15,304.1bn. This is a balance-sheet context measure, not a substitute for gross-debt and net-debt data.

### Collateral / liquidity

The current supplement does not include cash, inventory, receivables, current liabilities or committed facilities as separate approved metrics. A lender should therefore treat collateral and liquidity as **information pending**, not assume that total assets are liquid collateral.

### Conditions

FY2025 revenue was broadly flat/down 1.1%, while operating profit fell 13.8%. This suggests a margin and operating-leverage stress under mature-scale conditions. The sector can also face input-cost, channel-mix and trade-spend volatility; these must be tested with management data.

### Character / controls

Evidence governance is strong: source URLs, printed page anchors, confidence, comparability and restatement fields are retained. The FY2017 comparative caveat is visible. That control discipline reduces reporting risk in the portfolio case, but it is not evidence of MCH management quality in a live underwriting decision.

## 4. Cash-conversion diagnosis

The central credit question is the FY2025 CFO/PAT drop from 116.5% to 31.5%.

What is proven:

- accounting profit remained positive;
- operating cash flow weakened materially;
- the balance-sheet equity ratio increased.

What is not proven from this layer:

- whether receivables or inventory caused the shortfall;
- whether tax, provisions, supplier terms or one-off operating items explain it;
- whether cash was diverted to capex, acquisitions or other investing uses;
- whether the drop is temporary or structural.

Required bridge: CFO = PAT + non-cash items - working-capital investment + other operating adjustments. Request the full cash-flow note and monthly AR ageing, inventory ageing, AP ageing, tax payments and one-off reconciliation.

The stress screen is deliberately simple: it shows how quickly cash headroom can compress even while PAT remains positive. It is not a debt-service model because gross debt, interest and maturities are not in the approved supplement.

## 6. Proposed lender conditions

Before approving incremental exposure:

1. Provide a 13-week cash forecast with weekly actual-versus-plan tracking.
2. Provide debt maturity, interest-rate, covenant and undrawn-commitment schedules.
3. Set a cash-conversion trigger: investigate if CFO/PAT remains below 60% for two consecutive quarters.
4. Set a leverage/coverage trigger only after verified gross-debt and interest data are received.
5. Ring-fence material capex/acquisition commitments until the FY2025 cash bridge is reconciled.
6. Require a monthly working-capital action log with AR, inventory and AP owners.

## 7. Decision

**Conditional support / enhanced monitoring.**

Approve only ordinary-course facilities sized to verified liquidity needs, subject to the information and monitoring conditions above. Do not convert the 54.9% equity ratio into an unconditional “low risk” conclusion: the FY2025 cash-conversion signal is too important to ignore. Conversely, do not label MCH distressed without a debt schedule and cash-flow bridge.

## 8. Interview-ready answer

> “My credit view is watch / conditional support. MCH has a 54.9% FY2025 equity ratio and a profitable decade, but CFO/PAT fell to 31.5% from 116.5% while revenue was broadly flat and PAT fell 14.6%. I would not infer insolvency from that alone. I would request the debt schedule and a working-capital bridge, impose a 13-week cash forecast and use a 60% CFO/PAT monitoring trigger until the cause is resolved.”

## Limitations

- No gross-debt, interest, cash, maturity or covenant fields are in the approved eight-metric supplement.
- CFO/PAT is a screening ratio, not a debt-service coverage ratio.
- Stress outputs are illustrative assumptions, not company guidance.
- FY2017 uses comparative/corresponding columns from FY2018.
- This is a portfolio credit-screening exercise, not a lending decision.
